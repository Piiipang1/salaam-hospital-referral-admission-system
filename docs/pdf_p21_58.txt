2.4 Technical Background  
Salaam Hospital is a key healthcare institution serving the local community, offering 
medical services, outpatient care, emergency response, and patient admissions. Like many midsized 
hospitals in the Philippines, it caters to a growing population with div erse medical needs. With 
increasing healthcare demands, efficient management of patient flow, hospital operations, and care 
provider coordination is critical.  
Currently, hospital operations at Salaam Hospital are largely manual and paper -based. Patient 
registration, admission, and care tracking rely on handwritten forms, phone calls, or verbal 
communication. Records are often incomplete, illegible, or fragmented  across departments, 
personal devices, and offline logs. This results in delays in patient care, inefficient staff 
coordination, and difficulty in tracking resource utilization or patient history.  
A significant challenge is the absence of a centralized digital platform integrating patient 
admission, medical records, and care provider tracking . Information is scattered, making it 
difficult to monitor patient status, coordinate staff assignments, and generate accurate reports on 
hospital performance. Existing digital health systems like PAS, Appoluz HIS, and WAH focus 
mainly on appointments, ele ctronic medical records, or internal workflows. They lack a unified 
system that tracks patient admissions whi le monitoring the activities and assignments of doctors, 
nurses, and other care providers in real time.

Figure 3.1 Agile Methodology

3.2 Kanban Framework  
Kanban was selected as the Agile framework for the development of the Responsive Web-Based 
Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital. This framework emphasizes continuous 
delivery, visual workflow management, and adaptability, making it highly suitable for a small 
development team working with changing hospital requirements. Since healthcare systems often evolve 
due to policy updates and user feedback, Kanban allows tasks to be added, modified, and prioritized 
without the need for fixed-length iterations, ensuring continuous progress throughout the system 
development.  

=== PAGE 33 ===
28  
  
The implementation of Kanban in this project followed several core Kanban practices that guided the 
development process:  
1. Visualize the Workflow  
A Kanban board was utilized to visually represent all development activities of the system. Tasks 
were organized into columns such as To Do, In Progress, and Done. This visualization enabled 
the developers to easily track the status of features such as patient referral processing, admission 
management, and user interface enhancements, while also providing a clear overview of the 
project’s progress.  
2. Limit Work in Progress (WIP)  
To avoid task overload and ensure quality output, limits were applied to the number of tasks being 
worked on simultaneously. This practice helped the development team focus on completing 
critical modules such as referral approval and patient admission records before starting new tasks, 
thereby improving efficiency and reducing unfinished work.  
3. Manage Flow  
The movement of tasks across the Kanban board was continuously monitored to identify delays or 
bottlenecks in development. This allowed the team to address issues related to system 
functionality, database integration, or responsiveness early, ensuring a smoother and more 
efficient development flow.  
4. Make Process Policies Explicit  
Clear guidelines were established regarding how tasks moved between columns on the Kanban 
board. For example, a task could only move from To Do to In Progress once its requirements 
were clearly defined, and from In Progress to Done only after successful testing and validation.  
This ensured consistency and quality throughout the development process.  
=== PAGE 34 ===
29  
  
5. Implement Feedback Loops  
Regular discussions were conducted among the development team to review completed tasks, 
assess system performance, and address any issues encountered. Feedback from these discussions 
was used to refine system features, particularly those related to hospital workflows such as patient 
referrals, admissions, and record management.  
6. Improve Collaboratively and Evolve Experimentally  
The team continuously improved their workflow by identifying areas for enhancement and 
adjusting their development practices based on experience and feedback. This collaborative and 
adaptive approach allowed the system to evolve gradually, ensuring that it effectively meets the 
operational needs of Salaam Hospital.

 Figure 3.11  presents the Entity –Relationship Diagram (ERD) of the "Responsive 
WebBased Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital", illustrating the logical 
structure of the system’s database and the relationships among its core entities. The ERD 
demonstrates how patient information, clinical processes, hospital resou rces, and staff roles are 
interconnected to support efficient emergency care, referral, and admission workflows.  
The Patient entity serves as one of the central components of the system, storing essential 
patient information such as Patient_ID, name, sex, date of birth, contact details, address, and 
emergency contact information. This entity ensures that patient reco rds are accurately maintained 
  

=== PAGE 51 ===

and can be referenced throughout the triage, diagnosis, laboratory, treatment, referral, and 
admission processes.  
The Employee and Doctor entities represent hospital personnel involved in patient care and 
system operations. The Employee entity includes staff such as nurses and other ER personnel, while 
the Doctor entity stores information about licensed physicians, in cluding specialization, contact 
details, and employment status. These entities support role-based access and accountability within 
the system. The Admin entity represents system administrators who manage user accounts and 
system access, ensuring secure authentication and proper system maintenance.  
The Triage entity captures initial patient assessment data upon arrival at the emergency 
room. It is linked to the Patient, Employee, and Visit_Room entities, allowing the system to record 
triage level (Critical, Urgent, or Non -Urgent), notes, assigned roo m, and date and time of 
assessment. Associated with triage is the Vital_Signs entity, which stores recorded health indicators 
such as blood pressure, heart rate, temperature, and respiratory rate, providing clinical data 
necessary for medical decision-making.  
Medical evaluation and treatment processes are represented by the Diagnosis, Treatment, 
and Lab_Results entities. The Diagnosis entity records the identified medical condition, the doctor 
in charge, laboratory references, and diagnosis date. The Treatment entity is linked to Diagnosis 
and includes prescribed medications, dosage, frequency, and treatment duration. The Lab_Results 
entity stores laboratory findings related to the patient, including test type, results, file attachments, 
and dates conducted, supporting accurate and timely diagnosis.  
Hospital resource management is handled through the Room, Visit_Room, and Admit 
entities. The Room entity stores information about room types, bed numbers, and availability status. 
The Admit entity links patients to assigned doctors and rooms, recording ad mission type and

3.4.5 Architectural Design  
The system architecture of the “Responsive Web -Based Patient Referral and Admission 
System for Salaam Hospital” provides an overview of how the system’s components work together 
to support efficient patient referral, triage, admission, and hospital resourc e management. This 
section presents the arrangement of system layers and the interaction between users, the web-based 
application, the server-side processing layer, and the centralized database. 
  
Figure 3.12 Architectural Design  
The system follows a layered web -based architecture composed of three main components: the 
Client Layer, Application Server Layer, and Database Layer. This architectural design ensures clear 
separation of concerns, efficient data processing, and ease of system maintenance. The Client Layer 
consists of the web and mobile interfaces used by hospital personnel such as nurses, doctors, and 
administrators. Through this layer, users can perform tasks such as managing patient records, 
creating referrals, viewing admission details, and monitoring room availability. The client layer is 
responsible for presenting information to users and sending user requests to the application server. 
The Application Server Layer handles the core system logic and business processes. It processes 
requests received from the client layer, including validating user inputs, managing patient 
registration, handling referral workflows, assigning doctors, and processing patient admissions.

This layer also enforces system rules and access control before interacting with the database layer. 
The Database Layer is responsible for securely storing and managing all system data. It contains 
records related to patients, referrals, diagnoses, admissi ons, rooms, and user accounts. The 
application server communicates with the database layer to retrieve, insert, update, and maintain 
data consistency and integrity. Overall, this architectural design supports efficient referral and 
admission workflows, sec ure data handling, and scalable system expansion. By separating the 
system into distinct layers, the architecture enhances reliability, simplifies future modifications, and 
ensures smooth operation within the hospital environment.

3.6 Test Plan  
The test plan outlines the evaluation process designed to assess the usability, efficiency, and overall 
functionality of the Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital. 
The primary goal of the testing phase is to ensure that the system is user-friendly, responsive, secure, and 
capable of supporting accurate patient referral, triage, and admission processes. Testing aims to determine 
whether the system features meet the operational and informational needs of hospital admin istrators, 
doctors, nurses, and staff.  
3.6.1 Respondents and Locale  
The respondents for the system evaluation will consist of selected hospital personnel and 
Information Technology students who are familiar with web-based systems. These respondents may include 
nurses, hospital staff, and BSIT students who can provide both practical and technical feedback. A 
maximum of 30 respondents will participate in the testing process to ensure a range of user perspectives.  
The data gathering and system testing will be conducted at Salaam Hospital and within an academic 
environment where stable internet access and appropriate devices are available, allowing respondents to 
interact with the system efficiently and conveniently.  
3.6.2 Procedure  
The testing procedure will follow a structured and step -by-step approach to collect consistent and 
measurable feedback. First, respondents will be given a brief orientation and demonstration of the system’s 
features, including login, patient registration, triage recording, referral handling, and admission 
management.  
Second, respondents will be asked to perform specific tasks such as entering patient data, updating referral 
status, assigning doctors or rooms, and viewing admission records. After completing the assigned tasks, 
respondents will answer a usability evaluation questionnaire. Qualitative feedback and suggestions will also 
be collected to identify areas for system improvement.
