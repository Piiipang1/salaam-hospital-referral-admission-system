Chapter 1Introduction

Hospital Management Systems (HMS) have become essential in modern healthcare because they integrate and automate various hospital processes, including patient registration, admission, medical record management, referral coordination, and reporting. By replacing manual and paper-based procedures with centralized digital platforms, HMS enables healthcare providers to access accurate and up-to-date patient information, improve communication among departments, and support timely clinical decision-making. The implementation of Hospital Management Systems has also been associated with improved operational efficiency, reduced documentation errors, enhanced patient safety, and better quality of healthcare services. As healthcare institutions continue to adopt digital technologies, Hospital Management Systems play a significant role in improving both administrative and clinical workflows while ensuring more organized and efficient hospital operations (Al-Shorbaji et al., 2016; Khosravi et al., 2023).
Despite the benefits offered by Hospital Management Systems, many healthcare institutions continue to experience operational challenges due to the absence of an integrated digital system. Hospitals that rely on manual documentation and paper-based processes often encounter delays in patient admission, difficulty in retrieving medical records, poor coordination among healthcare personnel, incomplete documentation, and increased risks of human error. Similar conditions were observed at Salaam Hospital in Blis Papandayan, Marawi City, where patient admissions, medical record management, and internal specialist referrals are primarily handled through handwritten forms, physical records, and manual communication among healthcare personnel. Based on interviews and consultations conducted with hospital staff during the requirement analysis phase of this study, these manual practices make it difficult to organize patient information, monitor patient admissions, retrieve previous medical records, and efficiently coordinate internal referrals. Studies have likewise shown that ineffective health information management negatively affects the accessibility, accuracy, and quality of healthcare services by limiting the availability of reliable patient information and reducing workflow efficiency (Influence of Health Information Management Practices on the Quality of Healthcare, 2023).
To address these challenges, this study proposes the development of a Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital. The proposed system is designed to digitalize patient registration, medical record management, admission monitoring, and internal specialist referral processes through a centralized and role-based platform that can be accessed using different devices through a web browser. By providing organized patient records, real-time information access, and improved coordination among healthcare personnel, the system is expected to minimize documentation errors, reduce delays in hospital operations, and improve the overall efficiency of patient referral and admission workflows. Consistent with previous studies on healthcare information systems, implementing a responsive web-based hospital management solution can enhance workflow efficiency, strengthen communication among healthcare providers, and support better patient care through timely access to accurate and complete patient information (Khajouei et al., 2022; Windari et al., 2023)
1.1Project Context 
The Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital was developed to provide a centralized and digital platform for managing patient admission, medical records, and internal referral processes within the hospital. The system is designed to replace manual and paper-based procedures by integrating essential hospital functions into a single web-based application that is accessible through various devices with an internet connection. By utilizing a responsive web platform, the system enables authorized healthcare personnel to efficiently access, update, and manage patient information while supporting faster communication and coordination among different hospital departments.
The proposed system incorporates several core functionalities that support the hospital's daily operations. These include patient registration, electronic medical record management, patient admission monitoring, internal specialist referral management, bed allocation and monitoring, role-based user authentication, report generation, and notification features. The system allows hospital administrators to oversee patient admissions and referrals, while doctors and nurses can securely access and update patient information based on their designated responsibilities. Through these integrated features, the system promotes organized record management, minimizes redundant data entry, improves information accessibility, and enhances the efficiency of hospital workflows.
The implementation of the proposed system is expected to provide significant benefits to Salaam Hospital and its stakeholders. Hospital administrators will be able to monitor admissions, referrals, and patient records more effectively through centralized information management. Doctors and nurses will benefit from timely access to accurate and updated patient information, enabling improved coordination in delivering healthcare services. Patients are also expected to experience a more efficient admission and referral process through reduced processing time and better management of their medical records. Overall, the proposed Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System is expected to enhance operational efficiency, improve service delivery, and support Salaam Hospital's transition toward a more organized, reliable, and technology-driven healthcare environment.
1.2 Purpose and Description
The primary purpose of this study is to develop a Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital that will improve the efficiency of patient admission, medical record management, and internal referral processes through the use of an integrated digital platform. The proposed system aims to support the hospital's transition from manual and paper-based procedures to a more organized, accurate, and efficient method of managing patient information and hospital operations.
The study addresses the operational challenges identified at Salaam Hospital, including delays in patient admission, difficulty in retrieving and updating patient records, inefficient coordination of internal specialist referrals, and the risk of incomplete or misplaced documentation resulting from manual record-keeping practices. These challenges affect the efficiency of hospital workflows and may delay the delivery of healthcare services. To address these concerns, the proposed system provides centralized patient information management, electronic medical records, digital admission monitoring, and an organized internal referral process that enables authorized healthcare personnel to access and update patient information in real time. The system also incorporates role-based access, report generation, and responsive web accessibility to further improve workflow efficiency, communication, and information management across different hospital departments.
Through the implementation of the proposed system, Salaam Hospital is expected to achieve a more systematic and reliable patient referral and admission process while improving collaboration among hospital administrators, doctors, and nurses. By minimizing manual documentation, reducing processing time, and improving the accessibility of patient information, the system is expected to enhance the quality of hospital services, support informed clinical decision-making, and contribute to more efficient healthcare delivery for the benefit of both healthcare providers and patients.

1.4 Scope and Limitations
This capstone project focuses on the design and development of a computerized system intended to assist the hospital’s patient referral and admission processes. The study covers only the activities related to recording, managing, and monitoring of patient referral and admission information within Salaam Hospital. It also includes the coordination between hospital departments involved in referring and admitting patients. The project is primarily concerned with data management, documentation, and communication related to the referral and admission workflow of the hospital.
The system includes basic medical record management related to patient admission, internal referral, diagnosis, triage, and treatment documentation but does not support full laboratory systems, billing, or external health system integration.
This study is limited to the administrative and operational aspects of handling patient referrals and admissions. It does not include financial transactions, and billing. External hospital networks outside Salaam Hospital and third-party health institutions are also not covered in this project. The proposed system’s focus is on improving internal hospital efficiency in processing referrals and admissions, without addressing broader health information systems or government-level health data integration.

2.1 Review of Related Concepts

By clarifying these foundational ideas—such as hospital management systems, patient referral, admission processes, and responsive web design—this section establishes a common understanding that guides the succeeding discussions in this chapter.
2.1.1 Hospital Management Systems (HMS)
Hospital Management Systems (HMS) are comprehensive software platforms that automate and streamline the operational, administrative, and clinical processes of healthcare facilities. HMS integrates multiple modules, including patient registration, appointment scheduling, billing, laboratory management, pharmacy, and human resource management. By centralizing all hospital operations, HMS reduces paperwork, prevents duplication of records, and improves communication between departments. the implementation of HMS enhances hospital efficiency by allowing staff to access patient and operational data in real time, enabling informed decision-making and timely response to patient needs. For instance, a patient admitted through the HMS can have their medical history, lab results, and billing details immediately available to doctors, nurses, and administrative staff, which reduces delays and errors. Moreover, HMS supports reporting and analytics functions that help hospital management monitor resource utilization, track performance, and plan improvements in healthcare delivery. This system ultimately contributes to higher quality patient care and smoother hospital workflows.
2.1.2 Patient Admission and Discharge Systems
Patient Admission and Discharge Systems are specialized software solutions that manage the complete lifecycle of a patient’s stay in a healthcare facility, from admission to discharge. These systems handle patient intake, bed allocation, recording of medical histories, generation of discharge summaries, and coordination with billing departments. By automating these processes, hospitals can reduce patient waiting times, minimize errors in documentation, and improve workflow efficiency among healthcare staff. According to research, such systems enhance operational management by providing real-time updates on bed availability, patient status, and required treatments. For example, when a patient is admitted, the system can automatically notify nurses, doctors, and lab personnel about the patient’s condition and scheduled tests, ensuring timely care. Additionally, centralized recordkeeping ensures that all patient information is securely stored and easily accessible for future reference, supporting continuity of care and informed decision-making.
2.1.3 Integration of EMR/EHR with Hospital Management Systems
The integration of Electronic Medical Records (EMR) and Electronic Health Records (EHR) with Hospital Management Systems (HMS) allows hospitals to create a seamless, centralized digital platform that connects administrative and clinical processes. This integration enables real-time synchronization of medical histories, diagnostic reports, treatment plans, and billing information across departments. Studies indicate that such integration improves operational efficiency, reduces redundant testing, and enhances continuity of care by ensuring that all healthcare providers have access to accurate and up-to-date patient information. For example, when a patient is transferred from one department to another, doctors and nurses can immediately access previous test results, medications, and referral notes, minimizing delays and potential errors. Moreover, integrating EMR/EHR with HMS supports advanced analytics, enabling hospitals to monitor performance, plan resource allocation, and improve service delivery.
2.1.4 Digital Admission Management Systems in Hospital Operations 
Digital admission management systems in hospitals provide a centralized electronic platform that streamlines patient intake and replaces traditional manual or paper-based admission workflows. These systems often include features such as online pre-registration, real-time tracking of patient admissions, and integrated data capture at the point of service, allowing administrative and clinical staff to manage admissions efficiently. Research indicates that digital admission systems improve operational efficiency, reduce patient waiting times, and enhance coordination among registration, clinical, and administrative units. 
Hospitals implementing such systems report faster patient turnaround, while patients and families benefit from greater convenience and reduced waiting. Electronic admission platforms also minimize administrative workload, decrease documentation errors, and provide real-time access to bed availability, admission progress, and patient flow. For example, when a patient is admitted through the digital system, all relevant staff can immediately access their registration details and medical information, ensuring smooth transitions between departments. Integrating digital admission management into the proposed Hospital Management and Admission System for Salaam Hospital can therefore optimize patient flow, improve data accuracy, and support higher-quality service delivery.

2.2.5 Real-Life Implementation and Evaluation of an Electronic Referral System 
A study titled “Real Life Implementation and Evaluation of the e-Referral System SIPILINK” examined the deployment and early outcomes of an electronic referral platform designed to streamline intra-hospital referral workflows, enhance communication among clinical staff within the same hospital, and improve patient care pathways. 2.2.6 Lessons from a Web-Based Referral System for Acute Neurosurgical Referrals 
The article “Lessons from a Web-Based Referral System for Acute Neurosurgical Referrals. The system was developed to support intra-hospital specialist referrals, enabling physicians from different departments within the same hospital to electronically submit and track neurosurgical referral requests.
The study found that transitioning to an electronic referral platform resulted in more organized and searchable referral records, improved documentation quality, and reduced reliance on incomplete or illegible paper referrals. The system provided a centralized database where each internal referral contained detailed patient information and timestamps, thereby improving communication, traceability, and coordination among hospital departments involved in the referral process.
This study supports the objectives of the proposed Hospital Management and Admission System for Salaam Hospital by demonstrating the effectiveness of a hospital-based digital referral workflow. By centralizing referral data and automating communication within the hospital, the system can reduce documentation errors, improve response times, and enhance coordination among in-house specialists. These outcomes align with the proposed system’s goals of streamlining internal referral processes, improving patient management, and increasing overall operational efficiency.

2.3.1 Integrated Hospital Operations and Management Information System(iHOMIS)
The Integrated Hospital Operations and Management Information System (iHOMIS) was developed by the Department of Health (DOH), Philippines, to modernize hospital operations and optimize healthcare service delivery in public hospitals. Its main objective is to digitally centralize hospital processes such as patient admission, registration, billing, laboratory management, pharmacy coordination, and medical record maintenance. By doing so, the system allows healthcare staff to access real-time patient information, track the status of admissions, and make data-driven decisions efficiently. iHOMIS operates through a centralized database that integrates multiple modules, including admission and registration which collects patient personal information, assigns beds, and schedules treatments; medical record management which maintains electronic health records including past consultations, lab results, and referral history; billing and PhilHealth claims processing which automates fee computation, payment tracking, and insurance documentation; and reporting and analytics which generates operational and clinical reports to support hospital management and government compliance. The system has been implemented in several DOH public hospitals, improving operational efficiency, reducing human errors caused by manual documentation, and facilitating timely patient care. It is widely used by hospital administrators, doctors, nurses, and clerical staff, making it an essential tool for public hospital operations. This system is highly relevant to the capstone project because it exemplifies the integration of patient admission and history tracking in a centralized platform. The proposed Web and Mobile-Based Hospital Management System builds upon these concepts, providing web and mobile accessibility,
real-time updates, and a user-friendly interface for tracking patient records and admissions efficiently.

2.3.2 CNR Software Systems
The CNR Software Systems developed the Appoluz Hospital Information System (HIS) and Electronic Medical Records (EMR) to automate hospital operations and improve the efficiency of patient care in Philippine health facilities. Its primary purpose is to streamline workflows, including patient registration, admissions, medical records management, laboratory and radiology processes, pharmacy inventory, and billing, thereby reducing manual paperwork and minimizing errors. The system was designed as a comprehensive digital platform that integrates all major hospital departments and supports real-time data management. Patients’ medical information is digitized through EMR, allowing doctors and nurses to access historical records, vital signs, diagnoses, and e-prescriptions easily. The system also supports ICD-10 coding, automated billing, and PhilHealth or HMO processing to ensure regulatory compliance and accurate documentation. CNR Software Systems developed the platform locally in the Philippines, focusing on cost-effective solutions accessible to hospitals of various sizes. The Appoluz HIS incorporates features such as smart pharmacy inventory, laboratory and radiology result management, patient queuing, and financial reporting modules. It is DOH-accredited, which ensures that it meets national standards for e-health systems. Many hospitals and clinics in the Philippines, including Clinica Antipolo, SorianoLeyble Medical and Maternity Hospital, St. Therese Hospital, San Isidro Hospital, and Taytay Maternity and Child Care Center, have implemented the system. Reported benefits include improved workflow efficiency, reduced paperwork, better coordination among departments, accurate and faster billing, and streamlined access to patient information. The system allows hospital staff to monitor patient progress, track laboratory and pharmacy usage, and maintain a centralized database for easier decision-making. While there is no formal peer-reviewed study quantifying its impact, the adoption of Appoluz HIS has reportedly enhanced operational efficiency, improved documentation, and supported hospitals in providing more organized and reliable patient care.

2.3.3 WAH (We Are Health)
The We Are Health (WAH) Platform is a Philippine-based digital health solution designed to support health facilities and local government units (LGUs) in managing patient information, clinical workflows, and health program monitoring. Its primary purpose is to streamline hospital and clinic operations, improve record-keeping, and enhance patient care through digital solutions. WAH is composed of several modules, including WAH4C for clinics, WAH4H for hospitals, WAH4P for patients, and WAH4Interoperability for sharing patient information across health facilities. These modules enable electronic medical records, laboratory and radiology result management, pharmacy inventory, patient queuing, automated billing, and health alerts. The system was developed to be cloud-based and accessible, providing real-time data management and integration with national health programs. WAH supports interoperability between multiple health facilities, allowing patient information to be securely shared and coordinated across different clinics and hospitals. Health facility staff such as doctors, nurses, and administrative personnel are the primary users, while patients can access the platform for appointment reminders, health alerts, queuing, and feedback. The platform employs a “Train Now, Pay Later” scheme for LGUs and facilities with limited budgets, while some patient-facing features are expected to be free of charge. WAH has been reported to reduce manual documentation, improve workflow efficiency, and enhance patient follow-up, particularly for rural health units implementing national health programs. 3.1 Agile Model Methodology

Figure 3.1 Agile Methodology
The developers used Agile Methodology as the software development life cycle for this project. Agile is an iterative and incremental approach to software development that emphasizes flexibility, collaboration, continuous improvement, and adaptation to changing requirements. Unlike traditional development approaches where requirements are finalized before implementation, Agile allows developers to continuously review, refine, and improve the system throughout the development process.
Agile methodology was selected for the development of the “Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital” because the project required continuous communication, validation, and feedback from stakeholders, particularly hospital personnel who are the intended users of the system. Since the system was developed based on the operational needs of Salaam Hospital, changes and improvements in requirements could be identified during the development process. Agile provided the flexibility needed to accommodate these changes without affecting the overall progress of the project.
During the development process, the project was divided into several iterations or development cycles. Each iteration involved planning, requirement review, system design, implementation, testing, and evaluation of developed features. The developers prioritized important functionalities such as patient registration, admission management, medical record handling, internal referral processing, and user access management. After each development cycle, the system was reviewed and evaluated to identify possible improvements and ensure that the developed features aligned with the requirements of Salaam Hospital.
The Agile approach also encouraged collaboration between the developers, project adviser, and hospital stakeholders. Regular consultations and progress evaluations were conducted to gather feedback regarding the system’s usability, workflow, and functionality. These inputs helped the developers make necessary adjustments to improve the system before proceeding to the next development phase.
Through the use of Agile Methodology, the developers were able to create a responsive web-based system that continuously evolved based on user requirements and evaluation results. This approach supported the development of a functional, user-centered, and adaptable system that addresses the needs of Salaam Hospital in managing patient admissions, medical records, and internal referral processes.
3.2 Kanban Framework
Kanban was selected as the Agile framework for the development of the Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital. This framework emphasizes continuous delivery, visual workflow management, and adaptability, making it highly suitable for a small development team working with changing hospital requirements. Since healthcare systems often evolve due to policy updates and user feedback, Kanban allows tasks to be added, modified, and prioritized without the need for fixed-length iterations, ensuring continuous progress throughout the system development.
The implementation of Kanban in this project followed several core Kanban practices that guided the development process:
Visualize the WorkflowA Kanban board was utilized to visually represent all development activities of the system. Tasks were organized into columns such as To Do, In Progress, and Done. This visualization enabled the developers to easily track the status of features such as patient referral processing, admission management, and user interface enhancements, while also providing a clear overview of the project’s progress.
Limit Work in Progress (WIP)To avoid task overload and ensure quality output, limits were applied to the number of tasks being worked on simultaneously. This practice helped the development team focus on completing critical modules such as referral approval and patient admission records before starting new tasks, thereby improving efficiency and reducing unfinished work.
Manage FlowThe movement of tasks across the Kanban board was continuously monitored to identify delays or bottlenecks in development. This allowed the team to address issues related to system functionality, database integration, or responsiveness early, ensuring a smoother and more efficient development flow.
Make Process Policies ExplicitClear guidelines were established regarding how tasks moved between columns on the Kanban board. For example, a task could only move from To Do to In Progress once its requirements were clearly defined, and from In Progress to Done only after successful testing and validation. This ensured consistency and quality throughout the development process.
Implement Feedback LoopsRegular discussions were conducted among the development team to review completed tasks, assess system performance, and address any issues encountered. Feedback from these discussions was used to refine system features, particularly those related to hospital workflows such as patient referrals, admissions, and record management.
Improve Collaboratively and Evolve ExperimentallyThe team continuously improved their workflow by identifying areas for enhancement and adjusting their development practices based on experience and feedback. This collaborative and adaptive approach allowed the system to evolve gradually, ensuring that it effectively meets the operational needs of Salaam Hospital.

The ERD demonstrates how patient information, clinical processes, hospital resources, and staff roles are interconnected to support efficient emergency care, referral, and admission workflows.

Associated with triage is the Vital_Signs entity, which stores recorded health indicators such as blood pressure, heart rate, temperature, and respiratory rate, providing clinical data necessary for medical decision-making.

The Diagnosis entity records the identified medical condition, the doctor in charge, laboratory references, and diagnosis date. The defined relationships ensure data integrity, efficient information flow, and coordinated collaboration among hospital staff. This design enables the development of a reliable, secure, and efficient emergency referral and admission system that enhances service delivery and patient care at Salaam Hospital.

3.4.5 Architectural Design
The system architecture of the “Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital” provides an overview of how the system’s components work together to support efficient patient referral, triage, admission, and hospital resource management. This section presents the arrangement of system layers and the interaction between users, the web-based application, the server-side processing layer, and the centralized database.
Figure 3.12 Architectural Design
The system follows a layered web-based architecture composed of three main components: the Client Layer, Application Server Layer, and Database Layer. This architectural design ensures clear separation of concerns, efficient data processing, and ease of system maintenance. The Client Layer consists of the web and mobile interfaces used by hospital personnel such as nurses, doctors, and administrators. Through this layer, users can perform tasks such as managing patient records, creating referrals, viewing admission details, and monitoring room availability. The client layer is responsible for presenting information to users and sending user requests to the application server. The Application Server Layer handles the core system logic and business processes. It processes requests received from the client layer, including validating user inputs, managing patient registration, handling referral workflows, assigning doctors, and processing patient admissions. This layer also enforces system rules and access control before interacting with the database layer. The Database Layer is responsible for securely storing and managing all system data. It contains records related to patients, referrals, diagnoses, admissions, rooms, and user accounts. The application server communicates with the database layer to retrieve, insert, update, and maintain data consistency and integrity. Overall, this architectural design supports efficient referral and admission workflows, secure data handling, and scalable system expansion. By separating the system into distinct layers, the architecture enhances reliability, simplifies future modifications, and ensures smooth operation within the hospital environment.

3.6 Test Plan
The test plan outlines the evaluation process designed to assess the usability, efficiency, and overall functionality of the Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital. The primary goal of the testing phase is to ensure that the system is user-friendly, responsive, secure, and capable of supporting accurate patient referral, triage, and admission processes. Testing aims to determine whether the system features meet the operational and informational needs of hospital administrators, doctors, nurses, and staff.
3.6.1 Respondents and Locale
The respondents for the system evaluation will consist of selected hospital personnel and Information Technology students who are familiar with web-based systems. These respondents may include nurses, hospital staff, and BSIT students who can provide both practical and technical feedback. A maximum of 35 respondents will participate in the testing process to ensure a range of user perspectives.
The data gathering and system testing will be conducted at Salaam Hospital and within an academic environment where stable internet access and appropriate devices are available, allowing respondents to interact with the system efficiently and conveniently.

First, respondents will be given a brief orientation and demonstration of the system’s features, including login, patient registration, triage recording, referral handling, and admission management.
Second, respondents will be asked to perform specific tasks such as entering patient data, updating referral status, assigning doctors or rooms, and viewing admission records. After completing the assigned tasks, respondents will answer a usability evaluation questionnaire. Qualitative feedback and suggestions will also be collected to identify areas for system improvement.

CHAPTER IV
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
This chapter presents the results and discussion of the development, testing, and evaluation of the Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System for Salaam Hospital. The discussion focuses on how the objectives of the study were achieved throughout the system development process, including the requirement analysis, system design, development, testing, and evaluation phases. 
The first objective was achieved through data gathering and requirements analysis conducted with the assistance of Salaam Hospital personnel. Interviews and consultations were conducted to understand the existing manual processes involved in patient admission, medical record management, and internal referral workflows. The gathered information was analyzed to identify the challenges encountered by hospital staff and determine the necessary functional and non-functional requirements of the proposed system. 
The second objective was achieved through the design of the system processes, user interface, and database structure based on the identified requirements. Various software design tools, including Use Case Diagrams, Entity Relationship Diagrams (ERD), Input-Process-Output (IPO) diagrams, and system architecture designs, were utilized to illustrate the workflow, data relationships, and overall structure of the proposed system. The user interface was also designed to provide an organized and accessible platform suitable for hospital administrators, doctors, and nurses. 
The third objective was achieved through the development and implementation of the responsive web application based on the approved designs and requirements. The system was developed using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and React.js for the frontend, Node.js for the backend, and MySQL for database management. The developed system includes functionalities for patient registration, admission management, medical record handling, internal referrals, user access management, and monitoring of hospital processes. 
The fourth objective was achieved through functional testing and usability evaluation to determine the system’s performance, reliability, and acceptability. The system underwent testing involving selected respondents who evaluated its functionality, usability, and effectiveness in supporting hospital workflows. The results of these evaluations were analyzed to identify the strengths of the system and determine areas for possible improvement. 
This chapter is organized according to the stages of system development and evaluation. It presents the results of the requirement analysis, system design, implementation, and testing outcomes to demonstrate how the developed system addressed the operational needs of Salaam Hospital and fulfilled the objectives of the study.

CHAPTER V
SUMMARY, CONCLUSION, AND RECOMMENDATION
This chapter presents the summary of all the data collected in this study, the conclusions drawn based on the findings, and the recommendations. This study was conducted to address the difficulties experienced by Salaam Hospital in managing patient referrals and admissions through manual processes. Based on the interviews and consultations conducted with hospital staff, the researchers identified that the current paper-based procedures create challenges in organizing patient information, monitoring referral progress, and maintaining accurate admission records. The reliance on handwritten documents and manual recording increases the possibility of delays, incomplete information, difficulty in retrieving patient records, and challenges in coordinating information among hospital personnel. These issues highlight the need for a more efficient and centralized approach to managing hospital records and workflows.
During the data gathering phase, the researchers conducted interviews and consultations with hospital personnel, including administrators, doctors, and nurses/staff of Salaam Hospital, to understand the existing patient referral and admission procedures. The gathered information helped identify the current workflow, challenges encountered by users, and the necessary requirements for the proposed system. Furthermore, empathy maps were utilized to analyze the experiences, responsibilities, and concerns of different users, allowing the researchers to determine the essential functionalities needed to address the identified problems and improve the hospital’s workflow.
Based on the gathered requirements, the researchers proceeded with the system design phase by developing the system structure, user interface design, database design, and workflow models. Various software design tools, including Use Case Diagrams, Entity-Relationship Diagrams (ERD), Input-Process-Output (IPO) diagrams, and system architectural design, were utilized to properly define the components and interactions of the proposed system. The design process ensured that the developed system was aligned with the operational requirements of Salaam Hospital and provided a user-centered solution for managing patient referrals and admissions.
To address these identified challenges, the researchers proposed the development of a Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System that can assist hospital personnel in managing patient information, processing referrals, and monitoring admissions through a digital platform. The proposed system aims to improve record accessibility, enhance coordination among hospital departments, and provide a more organized method of handling patient referral and admission processes while reducing the limitations associated with manual documentation.
The development of the system began with the identification of critical operational inefficiencies at Salaam Hospital, such as delays and record management issues caused by traditional paper-based procedures. Guided by the gathered requirements and under the direct mentorship of the capstone adviser, Sir Mcfire, the technical implementation commenced. The application was built using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and React.js for the frontend, Node.js for the backend, and MySQL for database management, fulfilling the objective to establish a functional, centralized web architecture.
Following the implementation phase, the system underwent continuous evaluation and validation through regular on-site consultations and progress checks at Salaam Hospital to ensure it met client specifications and operational standards. Furthermore, the development of the system followed proper software engineering phases, including requirement gathering and the use of appropriate software design tools to ensure alignment with the operational requirements of the hospital.
To evaluate the functionality and usability of the system in achieving its goals, testing was conducted in two phases. The Alpha Test involved five (5) programmers who evaluated the technical structure and system performance, while the Beta Test consisted of hospital staff from Salaam Hospital who assessed its practical usability in real-world workflows.
The Alpha Test obtained an overall weighted mean of 3.66, interpreted as "Agree," indicating that the programmers considered the system technically sound, functional, and well-structured. Likewise, the Beta Test produced favorable responses from the hospital staff, with respondents expressing positive perceptions regarding the system's usability, ease of learning, functionality, and overall user experience. The findings, alongside successful stakeholder reviews, demonstrate that the developed system effectively met its overall goal and specific objectives, providing a practical solution for improving hospital operations.

5.2 Conclusion
Based on the findings of the study, the following conclusions were drawn:
The Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System successfully achieved its intended objectives by providing an organized, centralized, and efficient platform for managing patient referrals, admissions, and hospital records. 
The system improved the accessibility and management of patient information by allowing authorized hospital personnel to register patients, record triage information, monitor referrals, assign hospital rooms, and manage admissions through a single web-based application. 
The results of the System Usability Scale (SUS) evaluation showed that both hospital personnel and student respondents positively accepted the system. The Alpha Test obtained an overall interpretation of "Agree," while the Beta Test also produced favorable usability results, indicating that the system is easy to use, easy to learn, and functions effectively. 
The evaluation further revealed that respondents appreciated the integration of the system's functions, particularly in organizing patient information and simplifying referral and admission workflows. Although some respondents identified minor areas for improvement, the overall feedback confirmed that the system performs reliably and meets user expectations. 
Therefore, the Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System is considered a reliable and effective solution that can assist Salaam Hospital in improving operational efficiency, reducing manual processes, and supporting better patient management.

5.3 Recommendation
Based on the findings and conclusions of the study, the researchers recommend the following:
Salaam Hospital may adopt and implement the Responsive Web-Based Patient Referral and Admission System as part of its daily operations to improve the efficiency of patient referral, admission, and record management. 
Future developers may enhance the system by incorporating additional features such as SMS or email notifications, online appointment scheduling, barcode or QR code integration for patient identification, and automated report generation to further improve hospital services. 
Future versions of the system may include integration with laboratory, pharmacy, billing, and electronic medical record (EMR) systems to provide a more comprehensive hospital information management solution. 
Additional security measures, such as multi-factor authentication, encrypted backups, audit logging, and enhanced access control, should be implemented to strengthen data privacy and protect sensitive patient information. 
Future researchers are encouraged to conduct evaluations involving a larger number of hospital personnel and healthcare institutions to further assess the effectiveness, usability, scalability, and adaptability of the system in different healthcare environments.
The patient identification feature may be further enhanced through the use of a more efficient and secure identification method, such as a unique patient ID, barcode, or QR code, to facilitate faster patient identification and retrieval of medical records while reducing the possibility of duplicate or incorrect patient information.
Future versions of the system may include a patient referral tracking feature for patients referred to other hospitals. This feature may allow authorized hospital personnel to monitor the status and progress of external referrals, helping improve coordination, follow-up, and continuity of patient care.
